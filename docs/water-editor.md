# Water surfaces

`build/water.py` owns the water build. A lake is a connected water region in the class raster. Each lake starts horizontal at the median dry-bank elevation plus 3 cm. Bed samples never determine its shape when dry banks exist. Optional shoreline following blends in a broad, smoothed shoreline fit, without fixing the rim to the banks.

Look > Water offers per-lake absolute elevation, height offset, water beyond the outline, and shoreline following (0 = flat), plus course-wide lake-bed color, deep (tint) and shallow water colours, clarity (the depth the water goes from clear and shallow to dark and opaque in) and ripple strength. Settings use interior anchors so outline edits retain the selected lake's settings while its anchor remains inside it. A removed anchor appears as unmatched settings; reset it. Merging two tuned lakes requires removing one override.

The editor previews the same Python generator used by builds and look saves. Preview requests do not write. Saving publishes an immutable `water-<hash>.json` before changing `course.json.waterMesh`. Older assets are retained so in-flight readers remain valid. New bakes and design builds also publish water. Restart the Python server after updating its code.

The shore (`build/water.py`). The water mesh is cut at its shore by the build, with many vertices along the edge, so the region the water covers is geometry and the same at every camera distance; neither the depth buffer nor the shader's outline clip decides it. Water is where the lake's surface is above the ground, within the painted outline grown by `excess` (metres, 0 to 10 in the editor, default 0), in the parts that touch the painted lake. The shore is found on a fine grid (`SHORE_STEP`, 0.25 m) in the ground smoothed by a cubic B-spline reaching `SHORE_SMOOTHING` (3 m) either side, as the renderer smooths the relief, so it is a smooth line and not the lidar's noise. The mesh has a vertex about every 0.25 m along the shore, and fewer inside (a lattice that is coarser by powers of two with the distance from the shore, up to the old interior spacing); it is triangulated by Delaunay, less the triangles that are not on water. Smoothing makes the ground rounder than the one drawn, so the exact shore (where the drawn ground meets the water) is not quite where the smooth one does. The mesh is therefore a generous outline: it runs past the smooth shore by the most the drawn ground falls below the smooth one within the spline's reach (smoothed, at most `SHORE_OVERLAP_MAX`, 15 cm of ground) and `SHORE_OVERLAP` (3 cm), and the renderer cuts the water at the exact shore itself, per pixel, from the heights (see the water's body, below), so the shore is right however coarse the ground mesh is drawn. A first version set the shore back inside the exact one so that the water was never under the drawn ground; that left a bed-coloured strip, and at a distance the ground mesh (which drops below the exact ground along the shore, see below) still left a gap. Where the ground is above the water inside the outline, the shore is the ground's (the level decides it: lower it for banks to stand above the water); where the ground is below the water at the outline, the outline decides it, and `excess` lets the water go further. The exception is a bank only a few centimetres above the water *at the outline*, which no level setting hits exactly and which would otherwise leave a strip of bare bed between the water and the outline: there the surface is raised to meet the bank, by as much as the ground stands above it up to `SHORE_LIFT_MAX` (10 cm), fading out over `SHORE_LIFT_REACH` (1 m) inside the outline. The raised vertices are in the mesh's `y` (the normals stay up), so the water is flat in the lake and a few centimetres higher right at such a shore; a bank higher than `SHORE_LIFT_MAX` still holds the water back. The outline's signed distance is blurred by `OUTLINE_STAIRS` (0.3 m) to take out the grid's stairs, and no more, because blurring rounds the outline's tips inward. A lake with nothing above its level inside the outline has no water, and its mesh has no triangles.

In the viewer the water is drawn nearer the eye than it is, by a bias in depth: each vertex is pulled toward the eye along its own view ray, so it lands on the same pixel and nothing looks raised, only nearer in depth. The ground is drawn as a mesh that is coarser further out (0.5 m up to 51 m from the camera, then 1, 2, 4 m and so on) and its flat faces cut across a bank by up to about 2 cm for each square metre of the squares (`GROUND_FACE_ERROR_PER_SQUARE_METRE` times the spacing squared, times the exaggeration); the bias keeps those faces from hiding the cut edge, and is more for a shallower view (the same height error is further along the ray), at most half the distance to the eye. The first version raised the water by the same amount, which at a distance (8 m spacing and more) lifted it visibly off the ground; a bias in depth does not move it.

The water's body (`terrain/water/water.ts`). Depth at a pixel is the water's level minus the exact ground height there (`heightAt`, the heights themselves and any sculpting, not the ground mesh). Where it is 0 or less the water is not drawn, and it fades in over the first 6 cm of depth, so the shore is a soft exact line at every camera distance. With depth the water goes from the shallow colour to the deep one (`look.water.shallow` to `look.water.tint`) and from 80% opaque to 95%, by `1 - exp(-depth / clarity)` (`look.water.clarity`, metres; 1 m by default), so the bed shows through the shallows. The ground mesh is coarser the further the camera is, and it falls below the exact ground along the shore, as it shaves the narrow lip of the bank (lake 1 in the Augusta bundle, median under the exact ground at the water's edge: 0 at 0.5 m spacing, 9 cm at 2 m, 37 cm at 4 m, 57 cm at 8 m, 72 cm at 16 m), so an opaque water with a hard edge seemed to float above the ground; with the depth from the heights the edge is transparent there. A lake that is flat at the level all through (its ground flattened to the water) has no depth to show, and is the shallow colour at 80%.

The lake bed's top edge (the ground shader, `ground.frag.glsl`). Where the painted water meets other ground, the ground beside it carries on to the outline in its own colour, and the lake-bed colour comes in over the first `LAKE_EDGE_METRES` (1 m) into the lake. It comes in through the forest floor's soil noise (`soilEarth`), with the distance into the lake as its coverage, so the ground crumbles into the bed. That noise has its edges widened (`LAKE_EDGE_SOFTEN`) and a share of the plain ramp is mixed in (`LAKE_EDGE_SMOOTH`) so it is not as sharp as a forest floor. At the outline it is exactly the ground's colour and from 1 m in exactly the bed's. It changes the ground only on lake-edge pixels. The water surface and the clip of an older mesh use the water-class share (`water`), not the bed's (`bed`), so they are unchanged. The three numbers are in `terrain/limits.ts`, not in the look. It is seen where the bed shows: with Water surface off in the viewer, or where the ground stands above the water inside the outline.

The mesh file is kept small, because it is about as big as the height map otherwise (`build/water.py`): positions are written to a millimetre across the lake and a tenth of one in height, normals to 4 decimals, and the lattice inside a lake is never finer than `INTERIOR_MIN_STEP` (4 m) however small the lake, since the surface is smooth and the shader cuts the shore itself from the heights. Together that is a quarter off a mid-sized lake's file (more where the lake was small), with the level within 2 cm in the middle and exact along the shore. Normals are about a third of what is left and could be left out for the loader to work out from the triangles (not done: it is a change to the format both loaders read).

Mesh version 1 stores `lakes`: id, interior anchor [x,z], estimatedLevel, level, excess (absent in older files: 0), cut (true: the mesh is cut at its shore and drawn whole; absent in older files: false, a carrier the painted outline clips), indexed xyz positions, unit xyz normals, and triangle indices. All coordinates are course metres, x east/y up/z south. A mesh that is not `cut` (older) is a rectangular grid of at most about 128 segments on the longer axis that extends past the raster boundary; both renderers clip it with the terrain's spline/wobble/raggedness coverage, preserving that boundary, and so need class.png and the published look. A `cut` mesh needs neither to find its edge.

The raw WebGL preview uses the course sky color for reflections. Rally Club loads the identical mesh and adds its existing cloud reflections and fog. Neither renderer moves the exported vertices. Old Rally bundles without waterMesh retain the legacy runtime generator.

The lake basin (Heightmap editor, tool B). The water is one flat plane, and the terrain is a 1 m grid that has nothing to do with the painted outline, so where the ground at the outline is above the plane it hides the water (a rim of lake bed), and where it is below, the water floats over a gap. Level and offset cannot fix that when the shore itself varies. The basin sets the ground from its distance to the outline: inside, a bed that deepens from the waterline (Depth, Shore slope); at the waterline, 3 cm below the water, so the plane is never under it; outside, a bank that stands up as a lip (Bank lip) and gives way to the natural ground over the Bank width, keeping some of its bumps (Bank detail). Waterline shift moves the waterline in or out of the outline, because the drawn outline is smoothed. It works from the lake's current surface (the mesh's `level`), is shown live on the ground, and Apply keeps it as one undo step in the sculpt delta (`macro.delta`), so it is saved and built like any sculpting. Apply also fixes the lake's level in the look (`water.lakes.<id>.level`, offset 0): the estimated level is the median of the banks, which the basin moves, so an unfixed level would creep up with each build. To retune, Undo and adjust, or undo with Ctrl+Z; applying twice cuts twice. A lake whose outline sits on a slope (the shore ground varying by metres about the water) needs a wide bank, or a moved outline; the card says so. The maths is `web/src/terrain/sculpt/basin.ts`; the session is `web/src/scene/heightSession.ts`.

Validation: geometry, level/curvature behavior, independent lakes and anchor retention have CPU tests. Visual approval belongs to the user; no browser automation or e2e tests are required for this iteration.
